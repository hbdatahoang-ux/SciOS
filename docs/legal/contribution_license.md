# Contribution License Policy

**Project:** SciOS (Scientific Cognitive Operating System)

**Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Draft

Last Updated: 2026-07-09

---

# Overview

This document defines the licensing terms that apply to contributions
submitted to the SciOS Research Platform.

Its purpose is to ensure that all contributions can be legally
distributed, maintained, and incorporated into the project while
respecting the intellectual property rights of contributors.

This policy complements:

- LICENSE
- NOTICE
- CONTRIBUTING.md
- CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md

---

# Scope

This policy applies to all project contributions, including:

- Source Code
- Documentation
- Scientific Papers
- Figures
- Tables
- Equations
- Datasets
- Benchmarks
- Experiments
- Models
- Workflows
- Notebooks
- Supplementary Materials
- Metadata
- Tests
- Build Scripts
- CI/CD Configurations

---

# Acceptance of this Policy

By submitting a contribution to SciOS, you agree that your contribution
may be distributed under the licensing terms of this project unless
explicitly stated otherwise.

---

# Ownership

Contributors retain copyright ownership of their original work.

Submitting a contribution does **not** transfer copyright ownership to
the SciOS project.

---

# License Grant

Unless otherwise agreed in writing, contributors grant SciOS a
perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to:

- use
- reproduce
- modify
- distribute
- publish
- sublicense (where permitted by the project license)

their accepted contributions as part of the SciOS project.

---

# Project License

Unless explicitly noted otherwise, accepted source code contributions
become part of the SciOS codebase and are distributed under:

Apache License 2.0

Contributors should ensure they have the legal authority to license
their contributions under these terms.

---

# Research Artifacts

Research artifacts should include licensing information where applicable.

Examples:

- metadata.yml
- LICENSE
- citation.bib
- README.md

Artifact types include:

- Figures
- Tables
- Equations
- Datasets
- Benchmarks
- Models
- Workflows
- Supplementary Materials

---

# Original Work

Contributors represent that, to the best of their knowledge, submitted
work is:

- original; or
- appropriately licensed; or
- submitted with permission from the copyright holder.

---

# Third-Party Material

If a contribution includes third-party material, contributors must:

- comply with the original license;
- preserve attribution notices;
- identify applicable restrictions;
- document the source.

See:

docs/legal/third_party.md

---

# Patent Grant

Because SciOS is licensed under Apache License 2.0, contributors
provide the patent license granted by that license for accepted
contributions.

See:

LICENSE

for complete patent terms.

---

# Scientific Integrity

Research contributions should:

- accurately represent results;
- identify assumptions;
- provide reproducible methods;
- cite prior work appropriately;
- avoid fabrication or falsification.

---

# Contributor Responsibilities

Contributors should:

- follow project coding standards;
- follow documentation standards;
- update metadata where required;
- update registry files when adding artifacts;
- include appropriate tests when applicable;
- respect project governance.

---

# Review Process

Contributions are reviewed for:

- technical quality;
- scientific quality;
- documentation;
- licensing;
- security;
- maintainability.

Acceptance is at the discretion of the project maintainers.

---

# Contributor Attribution

Accepted contributors receive attribution through:

- Git history
- Pull Requests
- Release Notes
- Documentation
- Research Papers
- CITATION.cff (where appropriate)

---

# Contributor License Agreement (CLA)

At present, SciOS does **not** require a separate Contributor License
Agreement (CLA).

The project may adopt a CLA or a Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO)
in the future if required.

Any such change will be announced before enforcement.

---

# Developer Certificate of Origin

If a DCO is adopted, contributors may be required to certify that:

- they wrote the contribution;
- they have the right to submit it;
- they agree to the project's licensing terms.

---

# Withdrawal of Contributions

Because Git repositories preserve project history, accepted
contributions generally cannot be removed retroactively except in
extraordinary circumstances.

---

# Compliance

Project maintainers may reject or remove contributions that:

- violate licensing requirements;
- infringe intellectual property;
- include confidential information;
- violate applicable law;
- fail project review standards.

---

# Future Policy Changes

This policy may evolve as SciOS grows.

Material changes will be documented in:
