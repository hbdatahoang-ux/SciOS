# Extended Derivation of Effective Elasticity (SUP-0001)

## Introduction

This derivation provides the detailed steps leading to the **Effective Elasticity Equation (EQ-0001)**.  
It expands upon the brief explanation in the main manuscript, offering a full mathematical treatment.

---

## Step 1: Hooke's Law

Starting from the classical definition:



\[
\sigma = E \cdot \varepsilon
\]



Where:
- \(\sigma\) = Stress (force per unit area)
- \(\varepsilon\) = Strain (relative deformation)
- \(E\) = Young's modulus (material elasticity constant)

---

## Step 2: Constraint Conditions

Under constrained-induced elasticity theory (CEIT), external constraints modify the effective response.  
We introduce a constraint factor \(C\):



\[
\sigma = E \cdot \varepsilon + C(\varepsilon)
\]



---

## Step 3: Effective Elasticity Definition

Rearranging terms, the effective elasticity is defined as:



\[
E_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}
\]



This incorporates both intrinsic elasticity and constraint-induced modifications.

---

## Step 4: Limiting Cases

- **No constraints** (\(C(\varepsilon) = 0\)):  
  

\[
  E_{\text{eff}} = E
  \]



- **Strong constraints** (\(C(\varepsilon) \gg E \cdot \varepsilon\)):  
  

\[
  E_{\text{eff}} \approx \frac{C(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}
  \]
